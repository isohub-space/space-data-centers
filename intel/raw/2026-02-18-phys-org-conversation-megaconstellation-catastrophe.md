# Phys.org / The Conversation — "Too many satellites? Earth's orbit is on track for a catastrophe — but we can stop it"

## Reported (what the source says)
- SpaceX filed for **up to 1,000,000** satellites on **2026-01-30**; **~14,000** active satellites (Feb 2026); **1.23 million** satellites proposed across all projects.
- **> 50,000** debris pieces ≥ 10 cm already in orbit.
- If collision avoidance ceased, a major collision expected **every 3.8 days**.
- Night sky: 2021 estimate that **1 in 15** sky points would be a moving satellite at the then-proposed 65,000 satellites.
- Harms: astronomy (optical, radio), no unified space-traffic management, Kessler cascade, ozone damage from launches and from re-entry metals, loss of Indigenous sky traditions.
- Proposal: a **Dark Skies Impact Assessment** tied to licensing — evidence gathering, cumulative modelling, criteria, mitigation, independent review; "not a veto tool."

## Primary (where the underlying document differs or adds)
- Search snippet (Science, not opened): a modelling study of **16 planned constellations totalling ~1.7M satellites** found **6** cross the threshold into runaway debris growth. ESA model: **1.2M** objects 1–10 cm and **140M** < 1 cm.

## Derived (our arithmetic — formula shown)
- 1.23M proposed ÷ 14,000 active = **88×** today's population.

## Relevance to the Entrant
- Licensing regimes are moving toward cumulative-impact assessment; an operator that adds compute to existing EO buses adds **zero** new objects and can say so in every regulatory filing.
- Astronomy and Indigenous-sky objections are reputational risks for the megaconstellation players, not for the Entrant — but the Entrant must avoid being lumped in by using "orbital data centre" language loosely.
