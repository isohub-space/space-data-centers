# OBSERVER: What is IRIS²? — €10.6 B, ~290 satellites, full capacity 2030, no compute angle

## Reported (what the source says)
- Total project cost **€10.6 B**; **12-year** concession signed **16 Dec 2024** with **SpaceRISE** (Eutelsat, SES, Hispasat).
- Constellation: **18 MEO + 264 LEO-high + 10 LEO-low ≈ 290 satellites**.
- Timeline: design/development **2025–2028**; deployment **2029–2030**; **full operational capacity 2030**; exploitation **2030–2037**.
- SpaceRISE committed **10% of ESA's contract value to SMEs**.
- Integrates **quantum key distribution (EuroQCI)**; complements Copernicus as "the secure channel through which information can flow".
- **No mention of edge computing, in-orbit processing, EO data relay or ISL** in the column.

## Primary (where the underlying document differs or adds)
- Search summaries (not opened): an **implementation agreement on 7 Aug 2026** added **66 LEO satellites** → **348** in the main constellation; public share **€6.5 B** (incl. **€550 M** from ESA Partnership Projects), industry **>€4 B**; first launch envisaged **2029**. The 290 vs 348 discrepancy is the Aug-2026 enlargement.

## Derived (our arithmetic — formula shown)
- 10% of ESA's ~€550 M → **~€55 M of SME-reserved IRIS² work** — the only quantified IRIS² entry point for a startup.
- €10.6 B / 348 sats ≈ **€30 M per satellite** all-in — IRIS² is a sovereign govsatcom, not a cheap relay; EO-relay or hosted-compute on IRIS² is not planned and would be a 2030+ conversation.

## Relevance to the Entrant
- IRIS² will **not** be the Entrant's relay in this decade; the sovereign-relay story for EO edge compute in Europe runs through **HydRON/Kepler/Tesat**, not IRIS².
- The **SME quota** and the EuroQCI/secure-channel framing are the hooks if the Entrant wants to be on the IRIS² supplier list later (secure processing node on a sovereign network).
