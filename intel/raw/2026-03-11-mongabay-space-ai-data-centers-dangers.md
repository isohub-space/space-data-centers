# Mongabay — "Rush to put AI data centers in space poses poorly understood dangers"

## Reported (what the source says)
- **~14,000** active satellites today; projections of **60,000–500,000+ by 2040**; SpaceX seeking **1,000,000**.
- Re-entry pollution: particulates/vapour from burning space hardware **more than doubled 2020–2024, from ~366 t to ~887 t per year** (≈ "150 space shuttles burning up per year"). By 2040 stratospheric **alumina** may be enough to alter polar wind speeds and raise mesosphere temperatures.
- **CRASH Clock** (time to a likely catastrophic collision if avoidance ceased during a solar event): **164 days (2018) → 5.5 days (Jun 2025) → 3.8 days (Jan 2026)**.
- **> 300** successful orbital launches in 2025.
- Experts: Samantha Lawler (Regina) — *"Having a million satellites is completely unsafe for collisions."* Sarah Thiele (Princeton) — LEO is "largely a Wild West." John Plane (Leeds) — radiative balance, ozone, toxic elements. Leonard Schulz (Braunschweig) — 24 elements dominate atmospheric injection from orbital vehicles.

## Derived (our arithmetic — formula shown)
- CRASH clock deterioration 2018→2026: 164 ÷ 3.8 = **43×** worse in 8 years.
- Re-entry mass at SpaceX's plan: 1 Mt/yr launched with ~5-year life → **~200,000 t/yr re-entering** at steady state — **225×** the 2024 figure of 887 t.

## Relevance to the Entrant
- Debris and re-entry pollution are arguments against **megaconstellations**, not against a handful of EO-edge payloads on buses that already fly. The Entrant should position as "compute on satellites that exist anyway" — no incremental objects in orbit.
- EU Space Act debris/light-pollution rules (2028–30) will bite the million-satellite plans first; a small European operator is on the right side of that regulation.
