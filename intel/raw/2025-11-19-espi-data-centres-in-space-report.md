# ESPI — "Data centres in space: orbital backbone of the second digital era?"

## Reported (what the source says)
- Global data-centre market projected **€535B by 2030**.
- Drivers: AI compute demand straining grids, satellite data proliferation, solar abundance in orbit, digital sovereignty.
- Challenges named: launch cost, thermal management, immature in-orbit assembly — "problems European aerospace and technology industries excel at solving."
- Two-phase recommendation: near-term use ESA **GSTP** and **ARTES** as public-private testbeds; long-term a "European Space-Based Data Centre initiative" under **Horizon Europe Moonshot Projects (2028–2034)**.
- Landing page does not state a $/kg threshold.

## Primary (where the underlying document differs or adds)
- Search-snippet attributions to the full report (not verified on the page): launch must fall **below ~$400/kg** for viability; many ODC cost models depend on Starship at **$10M per flight**, which ESPI calls "unrealistic in the near-term." TechCrunch (Feb 2026) repeats the $10M/flight comment.

## Derived (our arithmetic — formula shown)
- $10M per 200-t flight = **$50/kg**; per 44 t demonstrated = **$227/kg**. ESPI's point: the $50/kg figure is the one most models assume.
- $400/kg threshold vs $7,000/kg rideshare = **17.5×** gap; vs F9 dedicated ~$3,000/kg = **7.5×**.

## Relevance to the Entrant
- Europe's own policy institute frames the opportunity as **2028–2034 moonshot funding** — i.e. a decade of public money for demos; the Entrant should be ESA/Horizon-fundable rather than purely commercial in its first phase.
- ESPI's sovereignty framing matches BCG's "sovereign AI workloads" use case; a European EO-processing operator is the natural first beneficiary.
