# Rocket Lab closes Mynaric acquisition at $155.3 M — Europe's main laser-terminal maker is now US-owned

## Reported (what the source says)
- Closed **14 Apr 2026**; final consideration **$155.3 M**, **$5.3 M above** original terms; **~2.3 M Rocket Lab shares** plus nominal cash.
- Original deal (Mar 2025): **$75 M upfront + up to $75 M** performance-based.
- German government approved the acquisition late **Mar 2026**.
- Mynaric's **CONDOR Mk3** terminals go on Rocket Lab's **36 satellites** for SDA's Tracking and Transport layers (**$1.3 B** of prime contracts).
- Mynaric's production problems had "nearly halted" OCT output; turnaround produced a "significant increase in terminals in 2025" with more planned for 2026.

## Primary (SEC Ex-99.1 and Payload — add)
- SEC release: aggregate **$155.3 M**, **2,277,002 shares**; Mynaric stays headquartered in **Munich** as Rocket Lab's first European footprint. Peter Beck: "Laser communication is a key enabler for satellite constellations."
- Payload (restructuring background): PIMCO-affiliated lenders put in **$95 M (Apr 2023) + $49.5 M bridge (Oct 2024–Feb 2025) + $25 M (Feb 2025) = $169.5 M**; total prior investment in Mynaric **>$300 M**; **equity holders wiped out** under the StaRUG process ("the shareholders came out empty" — co-founder Joachim Horwath); debt swapped to equity with **JVF-Holding**; SDA contract for which Mynaric supplied CONDOR Mk3 = **$515 M / 18 satellites** (an earlier Rocket Lab tranche).
- Neither source gives CONDOR Mk3 data rate or unit price.

## Derived (our arithmetic — formula shown)
- $155 M exit on >$300 M invested = **~0.5× money-back for the stack as a whole, 0× for equity** — the European laser-terminal champion was a value-destroying investment for its backers.
- 36 + 18 SDA satellites × 3–4 OCTs each (SDA Transport Layer typical, our assumption) ≈ **160–220 terminals** of committed Mynaric demand, all US-government.

## Relevance to the Entrant
- The **optical downlink/ISL enabler in Europe is now foreign-owned** (Mynaric → Rocket Lab US) or incumbent-owned (Tesat → Airbus). A European sovereign-latency pitch must either source from Tesat or accept a US-owned Munich supplier.
- Mynaric's story is the cautionary tale for **hardware-heavy space-comms startups in Europe**: >$300 M in, production hell, equity zeroed. The Entrant should be asset-light on terminals and buy, not build.
