# Morgan Stanley (via KuCoin flash) — SpaceX AI-compute valuation; Starship Q4 2026 is the key

## Reported (what the source says)
- Sum-of-parts per share: Enterprise AI **$165 (55%)**, Connectivity **$118**, Space **$8**, X & Grok **$8**; price target **$300** vs **$148.15** at the time.
- Assumes SpaceX compute deployment grows at **45% CAGR through 2040**, with **orbital computing becoming the primary capacity by 2032**, contingent on **Starship operational in Q4 2026**.
- Launch-cost path assumed: **$1,080/kg (2026) → $836/kg (2028)**.
- MS applies a **50% execution-risk discount** to the Enterprise AI segment; a Starship slip to 2029 collapses the AI narrative toward space + connectivity only.
- Search snippets (CNBC, not opened): MS mapped **43 companies** in the orbital-compute supply chain; sees near-term opportunity as **orbital edge-AI** (processing imagery/sensor data in orbit), not hyperscale replacement.

## Derived (our arithmetic — formula shown)
- $1,080 → $836 over 2 years = **−22.6%**, i.e. **−12%/yr**. Extrapolating −12%/yr from 2028 to 2035 gives 836 × 0.88^7 ≈ **$342/kg** in 2035 — above Google's < $200/kg and above ESPI's < $400/kg only marginally. MS's own curve does not reach Google's price point.
- "Orbital primary capacity by 2032" requires orbital compute > terrestrial xAI capacity within 6 years — inconsistent with Google's own lead saying not cheaper within 5 years.

## Relevance to the Entrant
- The most bullish institutional voice still frames the investable near-term as **orbital edge-AI on imagery and sensor data** — the Entrant's segment, named by the sell side.
- Any the Entrant fundraising narrative should cite MS's "edge-AI first" and avoid the 2032 capacity claim, which rests on a Starship cadence that has not materialised (3 flights in 2026 to date).
