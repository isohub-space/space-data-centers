# Wikipedia — New Glenn and Neutron status (Starship alternatives), Oct 2026

## Reported (what the source says)
**New Glenn (Blue Origin)**
- NG-1 **2025-01-16** Blue Ring Pathfinder — orbit success, booster landing failed. NG-2 **2025-11-13** ESCAPADE — success, booster landed. NG-3 **2026-04-19** BlueBird 7 — **second stage failed to reach orbit**, booster landed.
- **2026-05-28**: vehicle "No, It's Necessary" **exploded during a static fire at LC-36**, destroying the vehicle and severely damaging Blue Origin's only operational pad; no injuries; repairs or an alternative pad could take **over a year**. All planned dates "TBD". (Search snippets: cause confirmed 2026-08-05 as a BE-4 engine; CEO Dave Limp says it will fly again in 2026 via a hybrid horizontal/vertical CONOPS.)
- Capacity: **45,000 kg** to LEO (7×2); planned 9×4 variant **> 70,000 kg**. List price **$68–110M**.

**Neutron (Rocket Lab)**
- First launch "no earlier than **Q4 2026**" (Feb 2026 statement), slipped from 2025. Capacity **13,000 kg** LEO (booster recovered) / **15,000 kg** expended. Price **$50M**. Search snippet: Rocket Lab says the 2026 window is "narrowing".

## Derived (our arithmetic — formula shown)
- New Glenn: $68–110M ÷ 45,000 kg = **$1,511–2,444/kg**. Neutron: $50M ÷ 13,000 kg = **$3,846/kg**. Neither undercuts Falcon 9's ~$3,000/kg materially; neither is flying.
- Alternatives' 2026 record: New Glenn 1 flight (failed), Neutron 0. Starship 3. Falcon 9 carries essentially all heavy commercial lift.

## Relevance to the Entrant
- There is **one** operating heavy-lift provider; Bezos's orbital-DC timeline has no launcher behind it in 2026. The Entrant's launch risk is SpaceX manifest risk, which argues for small, rideshare-compatible payloads.
- Neutron's $3,846/kg at 13 t shows mid-lift does not solve $/kg; only Starship-class reuse does, and it is at 4 flights/yr.
