# WilmerHale — "Not Just Science Fiction: The Legal Reality of Orbital Data Centers"

## Reported (what the source says)
**FCC — four space-based data-centre systems accepted for review**
| System size | Orbit | Filing date (as given) |
|---|---|---|
| up to **1,000,000** sats, optical ISL | — | 2026-02-04 (DA 26-113, SpaceX) |
| ~**88,000** | SSO | 2026-03-13 |
| ~**51,600** | SSO | 2026-04-17 |
| ~**100,000** | SSO | 2026-07-31 |

- The alert names only SpaceX; the other three applicants are not named.
- **Space Modernization Order** (FCC 26-47, SB Docket 25-306, released 2026-07-23): a new
  licensing framework for space and earth stations; the FCC found it **"premature to adopt"**
  large-constellation standards before determining the required parameters.
- **Office of Space Commerce certification pilot** (launched 2026-08-20, implementing §5 of
  EO 14335): covers novel in-space activities **explicitly including orbital data centres**;
  expressions of interest due **2026-10-05**.
- Data law: GDPR applies to processing of EU data subjects "regardless of whether the processing
  takes place in the Union or not"; the US CLOUD Act reaches data in a provider's
  "possession, custody, or control" wherever located. Outer Space Treaty Arts. VI and VIII
  (state responsibility and jurisdiction) cited.

## Relevance to the Entrant
- The US has opened a **named authorisation path for orbital data centres** (OSC certification,
  120-day target per OSC material) while the EU Space Act is still a proposal — a US-based
  competitor gets regulatory clarity first.
- **GDPR + CLOUD Act** make data sovereignty a live selling point: a European operator under EU
  jurisdiction can offer what a US-controlled orbital node cannot — useful for sovereign EO
  buyers.
- The FCC declining to set large-constellation standards leaves the GW-scale filings in limbo;
  a kW-class payload on existing licensed buses avoids that queue.
