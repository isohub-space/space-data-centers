# "70,000 satellites, one big bottleneck": the industry wakes up to the data trap

## Reported (what the source says)
- **~70,000 small satellites** forecast to launch over the next decade, ~**$134 B** of investment (Novaspace, Nathan De Ruiter).
- Raw-data market grows only **6.2%** — the value is moving downstream to insights.
- Ali Younis (VP Sales, **Mynaric**): government is **70–80% of revenues**; manual ground-contact scheduling is like having to "pick this 5G tower at this time in order to call Ali".
- John Malsbury (CEO, AnySignal): **"Data stuck in space is meaningless."**
- David Proulx (CPO, SkyWatch): "We are still trying to sell raw ingredients to people who want a cooked meal."
- Hirokazu Mori (Warpspace US) on optical relay; **GHGSat** cited as a successful insight-first model; SDA as the anchor customer for optical.
- Consensus framing (per the piece): **store-and-forward is dead; the new mandate is "Time-to-Insight"**.
- No per-satellite data volumes, pass durations or downlink Mbps.

## Primary (where the underlying document differs or adds)
- not checked (Novaspace report paywalled)

## Derived (our arithmetic — formula shown)
- $134 B / 70,000 sats ≈ **$1.9 M per satellite** average — these are small, cheap satellites whose business case cannot absorb a bespoke ground segment each; shared relay + onboard triage is structurally implied.
- If raw data grows 6.2%/yr while satellite count grows ~7×, **revenue per raw byte collapses** — the margin lives in processing, wherever it sits.

## Relevance to the Entrant
- Independent industry voices (relay vendor, ground-software vendor, analytics vendor) now use the Entrant's exact vocabulary — **"Time-to-Insight"** — which validates the positioning but also means it is no longer differentiating on its own.
- Government at 70–80% of optical-comms revenue says the **first paying customer for latency is defence**, in Europe as in the US.
