# Lonestar — StarVault: "world's first commercially operational space-based sovereign data storage platform"

## Reported (what the source says)
- **StarVault** announced **2026-04-15**: sovereign data storage with **cryptographic key escrow** from
  space; first payload launches **October 2026 on Sidus Space's LizzieSat-4** (LEO, not lunar).
- **Second payload ordered from Sidus**, launch **2027**, for capacity/redundancy/coverage.
- Target customers: governments, financial institutions, critical-infrastructure operators (none
  named). Protection framing: cyber threats, natural disasters, geopolitical risk.
- Lonestar's **fifth mission**; "previously deployed four data centers to space, including two to the
  Moon" (IM-1 Independence, IM-2 Freedom).
- CEO **Steve Eisele**: "Demand for off-planet data security has exceeded expectations."
- **No capacity, price, orbit or power disclosed.**

## Primary (where the underlying document differs or adds)
- This is the primary. Note the pivot: the 2025 roadmap was **lunar L1 spacecraft 2027–2030**
  ([[intel/raw/2025-04-21-informationweek-lonestar-lunar-data-centers]]); the 2026 product is a
  **LEO hosted payload** — a cheaper, faster first revenue step.

## Relevance to the Entrant
- Precedent for **"sovereign" as the first paid orbital data service**, delivered as a hosted
  payload on someone else's smallsat (LizzieSat). Same go-to-market the Entrant can use for a first EO
  processing node: hosted payload, sovereignty + latency pitch, European government anchor.
- Sidus (US) is the host; a European EO node would want a European host (Reflex, OHB, Open Cosmos)
  for the sovereignty claim to hold.
