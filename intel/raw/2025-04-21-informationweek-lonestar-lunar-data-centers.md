# InformationWeek — Lunar data centers loom on the near horizon (Lonestar after IM-2)

## Reported (what the source says)
- **Freedom** payload launched **2025-02-26** on Intuitive Machines' **Athena (IM-2)**; lander **touched
  down on its side and powered down early**; Lonestar says storage and edge-processing functions were
  nonetheless **tested successfully**.
- Service: **DRaaS / premium backup / edge processing** from the Moon; capacity per future spacecraft
  "**multiple petabytes**".
- Customers: **State of Florida** (storage); **Vint Cerf** (edge-processing user); advisor **Caddis Cloud
  Solutions**.
- Funding: **$5M seed (2023)**; Series A "in progress" (Apr 2025).
- Plan: **six spacecraft 2027–2030** at the **Earth–Moon L1** point, each multi-PB + edge compute;
  capacity being booked.
- Chris Stott (then CEO): rationale is immutability against "climate change, natural disasters,
  human error, wars, nation states going after immutable data".
- Competitor note: Starcloud (ex-Lumen Orbit) "has raised over $20M" (as of Apr 2025).

**Cross-checks (opened)**
- Lonestar PR 2024-04-08: Freedom hardware **Phison** storage, **Microchip RISC-V** processor, Linux,
  **Skycorp** integrator, ARMAS radiation sensor; capacity on the mission "already sold out";
  first mission *Independence* flew on **IM-1, Feb 2024**.
- Blocks & Files 2025-02-14: founded **2018**; Ubuntu Linux; a planned 100+ TB "LiSS" server for ISS.
- Search-only (not opened): **$6.6M** round **2026-01-14** led by Atypical Ventures + The Veteran Fund;
  **Stephen Eisele** named CEO, Stott executive chairman; **$120M** preliminary agreement with
  **Sidus Space** for six LizzieSat cislunar spacecraft (Sidus PR 2025-06-23 opened confirms the $120M
  and Atomic-6 Light Wing 200 W/kg arrays).

## Primary (where the underlying document differs or adds)
- Lonestar's own 2024 release names **no customers** and gives **no capacity**; "sold out" refers to
  payload slots on Freedom, not revenue.

## Derived (our arithmetic — formula shown)
- Funding trajectory: $5M (2023) → $6.6M (2026) = **~$12M equity in 8 years** against a **$120M**
  spacecraft procurement — the Sidus deal is "preliminary" and must be financed by customer
  prepayments or a far larger raise.

## Relevance to the Entrant
- Lonestar is the **sovereign-storage** end of the spectrum (RISC-V, rad-tolerant, no GPU): shows
  that **"data sovereignty beyond Earth"** sells to governments (Florida) even at tiny scale and
  despite a failed landing. European sovereignty buyers are analogous.
- Not a competitor for EO edge inference; potentially a **co-tenant** (storage + processing on
  one node) story.
