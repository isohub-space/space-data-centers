# Satnews — Cowboy raises $275M and files for 20,000 orbital data centers ("Stampede")

## Reported (what the source says)

**Funding (restated)**
- Series B **$275M**, **$2B post-money**, lead **Index Ventures**. Total disclosed equity
  **~$325M** incl. the **$50M Series A 13 months earlier**.

**FCC filing "Stampede" (filed 2026-05-14)**
- Up to **20,000 satellites**; **700–1,000 km sun-synchronous**.
- Each: **20,000–25,000 kg**, **1 MW**, **~800 GPUs**; **optical** inter-satellite and downlink
  (no RF bands requested for data).
- Compute platform: **Nvidia Space-1 Vera Rubin module**.
- Rocket upper stage and data center = **one integrated vehicle** ("eliminating redundant mass").
- Design "incomplete; licence modification required before service".
- **First proprietary launch before end of 2028.**

## Primary (where the underlying document differs or adds)
- not checked (FCC IBFS filing not opened).

## Derived (our arithmetic — formula shown)
- Full constellation power: 20,000 × 1 MW = **20 GW** — same headline as Starcloud's "20 GW"
  ambition (IE 2026-08-23) and 1/5 of SpaceX's stated 100 GW/yr.
- Full constellation mass: 20,000 × 22,500 kg = **450,000 t** — more than the **370,000 t** Google
  says Starship must fly over a decade to reach $200/kg (see
  [[intel/raw/2026-10-01-techcrunch-google-suncatcher-1800-starship-launches]]). The filing
  implicitly assumes a launch market that does not yet exist.

## Relevance to the Entrant
- Three US filings (SpaceX 1M, Starcloud 88k, Blue Origin 51.6k, Cowboy 20k) all stake out
  **SSO shells 500–1,800 km** — the dawn-dusk orbit an EO-processing node also wants. Spectrum and
  slot congestion in SSO is now a **near-term European regulatory issue**, not a 2035 one.
- All-optical downlink is becoming the ODC default; a small node that still relies on X-band
  downlink will be the odd one out by 2028.
