# On-orbit data centers: mapping the leaders in space-based AI computing (Cutter, Sep 2025)

## Reported (what the source says)
- Timeline anchors: NASA–HPE collaboration since **2017**; **Spaceborne Computer-2** supported **>24 experiments** on ISS; **AxDCU-1** delivered to ISS **Aug 2024** (SpaceX CRS-33); **Axiom** plans two orbital data-centre nodes in **2025**; **Starcloud-2** targeted **2026**; **Φsat-2** launched Aug 2024 (Transporter-11); **China's Three-Body Computing Constellation** first **12 satellites 14 May 2025**, target **2,800 satellites**, **"1,000 peta-operations/s"** aggregate (Zhejiang Lab).
- European/near-European players mapped: **ReOrbit** (Finland; ESA InCubed; demo satellite 2025), **Kepler** (optical relay + Axiom nodes), **Space Compass** (JV, 2022).
- No funding, power-class or cost/energy figures.

## Derived (our arithmetic — formula shown)
- 1,000 POPS / 2,800 sats ≈ **0.36 POPS = 360 TOPS per satellite** — i.e. China's "computing constellation" is EDGX-Morus-class compute per satellite (≥250 TOPS), not data-centre racks. Useful calibration: the headline "exascale constellation" is thousands of edge nodes.

## Relevance to the Entrant
- Independent analyst framing already treats **EO edge nodes (Φsat-2, ReOrbit) and relay-hosted compute (Kepler/Axiom) as the same "on-orbit data centre" category** — the Entrant's "in-orbit data centre for EO" is a recognised sub-segment, not a stretch.
- Only two European names (ReOrbit, Kepler-via-ESA) appear in a global leaders map — the **European slot is thinly occupied**.
