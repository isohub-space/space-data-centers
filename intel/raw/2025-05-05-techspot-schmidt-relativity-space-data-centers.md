# TechSpot — "Former Google CEO Eric Schmidt wants to put data centers in space"

## Reported (what the source says)
- Schmidt acquired a controlling stake in **Relativity Space** and became CEO (March 2025); financial terms not given (search snippets say ~$800M invested).
- Rationale (from his congressional remarks): *"These things are industrial at a scale that I have never seen in my life."* Average US nuclear plant ≈ **1 GW**; some data centres planned at **~10 GW**; the industry needs **29 GW** more "within years" and **67 GW** more by **2030**.
- When Eric Berger speculated the purchase was about orbital data centres, Schmidt replied **"Yes."**
- **Terran R**: partially reusable, up to **33,500 kg** to LEO (expendable; 23,500 kg reusable per search snippet).

## Primary (where the underlying document differs or adds)
- not checked. Terran R has not flown as of the sources read.

## Derived (our arithmetic — formula shown)
- 67 GW by 2030 ÷ (Turyshev 34–59 kg/kW) = **2.3–4.0 Mt** to orbit if met in space — **~700–1,200 years** of 2025's global launch mass (~3,300 t). The rationale is a terrestrial-grid argument, not an orbital-feasibility argument.

## Relevance to the Entrant
- Schmidt's bet is on *owning a launcher* as the moat — consistent with Edge Aerospace's view that only vertical integration (SpaceX) can do GW compute. The Entrant's moat must therefore be data/insight, not infrastructure.
- A third billionaire-backed launcher aiming at orbital compute (after SpaceX, Blue Origin) raises the chance of cheaper rideshare in the 2030s but changes nothing before Terran R flies.
