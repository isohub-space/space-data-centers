# Gizmodo — "Starship Has Finally Gone Orbital. Now Comes the Hard Part"

## Reported (what the source says)
- **Flight 14, 2026-09-28**, Starbase: first Starship to reach orbit. One **Raptor 3** engine shut down during ascent; controllers authorised a single-engine burn to complete insertion.
- Deployed **26 Starlink V3** satellites — first operational payload. Mission cut from planned **10 h** to **< 3 h**; ship splashed down in the northern Pacific.
- Raptor 3 engines "have malfunctioned on all three flights where they were deployed" (Flights 12–14).
- **Flight 15** NET **2026-10-19** (FAA estimate), first attempt to **catch the ship** with the tower arms; Musk gives **50–60%** odds. **Flight 16** could fly **2026-10-30** from Kennedy Space Center.
- Musk: *"Starship is 2 to 3 years away from hourly flights."*
- NASA Artemis 3 still targets 2027.

## Primary (where the underlying document differs or adds)
- Wikipedia (Flight 14): liftoff 12:48:59 UTC; Ship 41 / Booster 21 (Block 3); orbit **262–275 km, 30.5°**; one of 33 booster Raptors and one of three RVacs failed; ship flight ~3 h 8 min; 3 of the 26 V3s carried cameras.

## Derived (our arithmetic — formula shown)
- Starship cadence: **2025 = 5 flights** (7–11); **2026 YTD (Oct 4) = 3 flights** (12–14). Google's $200/kg case needs **~180/yr**; SpaceX's FCC mass rate needs **~5,000/yr**. Current run-rate is **≈ 4/yr**.
- Musk's "hourly flights in 2–3 years" vs his Feb 2026 "space is the lowest-cost AI compute in 2–3 years": both now date to 2028–2029.

## Relevance to the Entrant
- Starship is real but at a test cadence; rideshare on Falcon 9 ($7,000/kg) remains the Entrant's price reality for the planning horizon.
- The Starlink V3 deployment is the first evidence of the 200-t-class bus that SpaceX's orbital-compute plan depends on; watch Flights 15–16 for the ship-catch, which is the gate to the price curve.
