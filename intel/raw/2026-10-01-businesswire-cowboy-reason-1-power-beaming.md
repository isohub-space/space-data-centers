# Cowboy Space — Reason-1 launched to demonstrate laser power beaming from orbit (Transporter-18)

## Reported (what the source says)
- **Reason-1**, Cowboy's first satellite (work begun as Aetherflux), launched **2026-10-01** on SpaceX
  **Transporter-18**.
- Mission: collect solar energy in orbit and transmit it to a ground receiver with a
  **"kilowatt-class laser"**; first in-orbit test of the **optical and thermal technologies "central
  to Cowboy's compute plans"**; tools and design principles feed the **optical communications
  network** of its planned orbital data centres.
- Cowboy designed and built the laser systems, a large-aperture optical telescope and the
  beam-steering architecture in-house.
- **Reason-2 (2027)**: optical data transmission space-to-Earth "at near-record rates".
- Cowboy is developing a **purpose-built rocket whose upper stage is designed to remain in orbit
  as a 1-megawatt data center**.
- Support: the US DoD **Operational Energy Capability Improvement Fund (OECIF)** provided
  "multi-year support" (hiring, components, design). Investors named: Index Ventures,
  Breakthrough Energy Ventures, Construct Capital, Interlagos, IVP. Nvidia named as Space-1 Vera
  Rubin module partner.
- No laser power, wavelength, aperture, mass, orbit or delivered-power figure is given in the
  release.
- *(snippet, not opened)*: beaming attempt "as soon as late October", 10–20 m ground spot,
  **30–100 W** delivered.

## Derived (our arithmetic — formula shown)
- If the snippet figures hold: delivered/emitted = 30–100 W / ~1,000 W ≈ **3–10% end-to-end** for a
  "kilowatt-class" source (assumes 1 kW emitted; not disclosed). That is a technology
  demonstration, not an energy product.

## Relevance to the Entrant
- Cowboy's first flight is **power beaming and optics, not compute**; its MW node remains end-2028.
  Tier-A players are still flying enabling-technology demos, not orbital compute for customers.
- DoD operational-energy money behind a GW-thesis company confirms **defence is the first payer**
  even at the GW end of the market.
- Reason-2's optical downlink (2027) is a capability the Entrant would buy, not build.
