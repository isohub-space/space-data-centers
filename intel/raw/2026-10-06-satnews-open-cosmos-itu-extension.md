# Satnews — Open Cosmos seeks one-year ITU regulatory extension after missing constellation deployment milestones

## Reported (what the source says)
- Open Cosmos asked the **ITU Radio Regulations Board** for a **one-year extension** after missing the **50%
  deployment threshold** on two spectrum filings, at the **June and September 2026** milestone deadlines
  *(reported)*.
- The two filings (originally **Rivada Space Networks**'s, folded into Open Cosmos's plans) cover a **576-satellite**
  constellation at **1,050 km** — **ConnectedCosmos**, a multi-layer LEO system for dual-use **Ka-band broadband
  and secure data transport** *(reported)*.
- **> 50 satellites fully built and in storage**, awaiting launch manifests and integration slots *(reported)*.
- If granted: **first six satellites before end-2026**; **96 in orbit by late 2028** *(reported)*.
- Platform includes **onboard compute**, software-defined comms payloads and **optical inter-satellite links**
  *(reported)*.
- RRB review at its **November 2026** meeting *(reported)*.

## Primary (what the underlying paper / filing says — where it differs)
- RRB submission not opened.

## Derived (our arithmetic — formula shown)
- 50% milestone on 576 satellites = 0.5 × 576 = **288** satellites required in orbit; plan if extended = 96 by
  late 2028 → 96 / 288 = **33%** of the threshold (*derived*), so even the extended plan does not meet the
  original milestone on its own — the board would have to accept a phased reading or a reduced filing.
- Satellites built but unlaunched (> 50) vs first-year plan (6): launch access, not manufacturing, is the
  binding constraint (*derived*, from the two reported figures).

## Relevance to the Entrant
- Europe's best-funded small-sat builder (€300M, Sep 2026 —
  [[intel/raw/2026-09-14-europeanspaceflight-open-cosmos-300m]]) has **50+ satellites on the ground for lack of
  launch slots**. This is the same launch-scarcity signal the GW players report, now hitting a European
  builder of onboard-compute platforms.
- For the Entrant: **secure the launch slot before the bus**; hosted payloads on an operator who already holds
  manifests (or a relay operator) reduce the risk that is stalling Open Cosmos.
- Open Cosmos's pivot toward a 576-sat Ka-band/secure-transport constellation makes it a potential **relay /
  backhaul partner** (onboard compute + OISL), not only a bus supplier.
