# Via Satellite — SpaceX acquires xAI to pursue orbital data-centre constellation

## Reported (what the source says)
- SpaceX acquired xAI (incl. X) to build "the most ambitious, vertically-integrated innovation engine on (and off) Earth."
- FCC filing (Via Satellite dates it **Jan 8, 2026**; Fortune/phys.org say **Jan 30, 2026**): constellation of **up to 1,000,000** satellites, **500–2,000 km** altitude, **30° and SSO** inclinations, different versions per shell, solar-powered, optically linked.
- Capacity claim: launching **1 million tonnes/yr** → **100 kW of compute per tonne** → **100 GW/yr** of AI compute added.
- Musk: *"Freed from the constraints of terrestrial deployment, within a few years the lowest cost to generate AI compute will be in space."*
- No deployment timeline in the filing; analysts tie it to the IPO narrative; latency and hardware reliability flagged.

## Primary (where the underlying document differs or adds)
- Fortune/Bloomberg (2026-02-01, Pashankar & Grush): filing calls the system "a first step towards becoming a Kardashev II-level civilization"; Musk at Davos: "a no-brainer", lowest-cost AI location within **2–3 years**; Starship is the launch vehicle.
- Al Jazeera (2026-02-03): Musk — Starship "aims to one day launch one flight per hour with a 200-tonne payload"; "In the long term, space-based AI is obviously the only way to scale."
- Press coverage of the later "Starmind"/AI1 satellite (≈150 kW peak, ~70 m tip-to-tip, two prototypes early 2027, ~1 GW/yr by late 2027) comes from search snippets only — not opened, not relied on.

## Derived (our arithmetic — formula shown)
- 1 Mt/yr ÷ 200 t per Starship = **5,000 Starship flights/yr** ≈ **14/day**. 2025 actual: 5 Starship flights; 2026 to date: 3.
- 100 GW ÷ 1,000,000 sats = **100 kW per satellite** average at full build-out, i.e. **~1 t each**.

## Relevance to the Entrant
- SpaceX's design point (100 kW, ~1 t, laser-linked to Starlink) is a general-compute node; the Entrant's EO-edge node is sensor-adjacent and 10–100× smaller. They do not compete for the same customer in this decade.
- The "Starlink as backhaul" architecture is relevant: the Entrant's downlink bottleneck could be solved by buying Starlink laser terminals (Starcloud already did — see Starcloud note).
