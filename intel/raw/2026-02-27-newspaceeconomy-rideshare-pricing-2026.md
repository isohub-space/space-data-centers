# New Space Economy — Satellite ridesharing market analysis 2026 (SpaceX price list)

## Reported (what the source says)
- **Transporter (SSO) 2026:** **$350,000 for up to 50 kg = $7,000/kg**, applying from Transporter-16 (NET April 2026) onward.
- Price history: 2019 announcement ~**$5,000/kg** (150 kg minimum); Oct 2022 **$5,500/kg** ($275k/50 kg); 2024–early 2025 ~**$6,000/kg** ($300k); mid/late 2025 ~**$6,500/kg** ($325k); 2026 **$7,000/kg** ($350k).
- Bandwagon (mid-inclination) 2026 price not separately given; Twilight (dawn-dusk SSO) follows Transporter rates.
- Dedicated Falcon 9 catalog price **~$69.85M**.
- Market: global launch services **$4.91B (2024) → ~$10.98B (2032)**; SpaceX 2023 Transporter revenue est. **$50–80M**.

## Primary (where the underlying document differs or adds)
- Payload (2023-03-01): price rose from **$5,500/kg (2022)** to **$6,500/kg (2023)**, +18% YoY after +10% the year before; two-year wait for a rideshare slot at the time. Payload's 2023 figure ($6,500) is a year ahead of NSE's timeline ($6,500 in 2025) — the two sources disagree on when each step happened; direction and 2026 endpoint agree.
- SpaceX's own page not readable; $7,000/kg is unverified against the primary.

## Derived (our arithmetic — formula shown)
- Rideshare price trend 2019→2026: 5,000 → 7,000 = **+40% in 7 years (~+5%/yr)** — launch prices for small payloads are **rising**, not falling, while the orbital-DC thesis assumes a 35× fall.
- Dedicated F9: $69.85M ÷ 22,800 kg (expendable LEO) = **$3,064/kg**; ÷ ~17,500 kg (reusable) ≈ **$3,990/kg**. Press figures of $2,700–3,600/kg sit in this range.
- The Entrant reference: a 100 kg EO-compute sat costs **$700k** to launch on Transporter in 2026; at Google's $200/kg it would be $20k — launch is **not** the dominant cost for a smallsat, the payload is.

## Relevance to the Entrant
- The Entrant's unit economics should be built on **$7,000/kg SSO rideshare** with annual increases, not on Starship projections; any slide using <$1,000/kg is a 2030s claim.
- Twilight (dawn-dusk SSO) is the orbit Google chose for Suncatcher and the one EO constellations use — the Entrant can co-manifest on the same missions as the hyperscaler's prototypes.
