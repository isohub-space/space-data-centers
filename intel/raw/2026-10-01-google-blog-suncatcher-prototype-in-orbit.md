# Google — "Project Suncatcher prototype satellite is in orbit"

## Reported (what the source says)
- Published 2026-10-01, signed by Travis Beals. Google's first Project Suncatcher prototype satellite launched on **SpaceX Transporter-18** and is "in orbit, operating as expected"; contact confirmed.
- Built in partnership with **Planet**.
- Framed as "a long-term research moonshot exploring whether space could one day host scalable machine learning infrastructure."
- Stated purpose of this flight: gather data on how TPUs handle **spaceflight stress, radiation exposure and thermal extremes**. Quote: *"Some things can only be tested in space."*
- Post announces the **peer-reviewed paper is now published in *Joule*** (link goo.gle/suncatcher-joule).
- The post itself does **not** give TPU count, power, model, burst duration, cost-per-kg claims or a 2027/2028 timeline — those come from press coverage (see NPR note: four Trillium TPUs, Gemma inference in 15-minute bursts; two-satellite laser-link demo in 2027).

## Relevance to the Entrant
- The hyperscaler's own framing is "moonshot", "long-term", "research" — not a product. The near-term commercial space-compute market is not Google-vs-the Entrant; it is EO/edge inference on small buses, which is exactly the Entrant's lane.
- Planet (an EO company) is the bus supplier and operator — the clearest signal yet that EO operators see on-orbit compute as adjacent to their core business. Watch Planet for a "compute as a service on our bus" product.
